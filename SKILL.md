## Before You Start — Know Who You're Writing For

Before writing or auditing any post, ask (or check context) for:
- The user's **profession / niche** (e.g. CA, engineer, founder, marketer, designer)
- Their **primary LinkedIn themes** (2–3 core topics they post about)
- Their **target audience** (who they want to reach — recruiters, clients, peers, students, etc.)
- Their **goal for this post** (saves, inbound, thought leadership, job search, brand deals)
- Their **preferred voice** (formal, casual, story-driven, data-heavy, etc.)

If you have none of this context, ask before writing. A post built on wrong assumptions is worse than no post at all.

## How the 2026 LinkedIn Algorithm Actually Works

### The Rebuild (What Changed)
LinkedIn rebuilt its feed using a 150-billion-parameter foundation model called 360Brew. It replaced dozens of older fragmented ranking systems with one unified reasoning engine. It reads the full semantic meaning of every post like a human editor would, and matches content to users based on interest graphs — not follower graphs. Hashtags barely matter. What you write about, how deeply you write about it, and whether *you* are a credible person to write about it — that is what determines reach.

### The 360Brew Four-Point Coherence Check
Before distributing any post, 360Brew runs a coherence check across four dimensions simultaneously. All four need to point in the same direction:

1. **Your Profile** — Does your headline, About section, and experience tell one clear story? Does it confirm you are a credible expert in the topic you are posting about?
2. **Your Network** — Are your connections the same people your content is designed to serve? Leftover connections from a different professional era dilute distribution because the algorithm has no relevant audience to show your content to.
3. **Your Engagement Patterns** — What content do you comment on, save, and share outside your own posts? This activity is tracked. If it matches your niche, it reinforces your topical identity to 360Brew.
4. **Your Content** — Does the post align with the story your profile, network, and engagement tell?

If all four are coherent: the post gets distributed. If they are misaligned: 360Brew has no reason to show your content to anyone.

**Skill rule:** Before writing or auditing any post, check if the topic matches the user's established profile and niche. A misaligned post does not just underperform — it can confuse the algorithm's categorization of the entire account.

### The 4 Stages of Distribution
1. **Quality gate** — Filters spam, engagement bait, obviously AI-written content, and low-effort posts. Post must pass this first.
2. **Golden hour test** — Shown to a small relevant audience. Algorithm tracks: dwell time, "see more" clicks, saves, and comment quality. Not comment count — quality. This window is 30–60 minutes.
3. **Interest-based expansion** — If it performs, the post goes beyond followers to users whose interest graphs match the topic. Distribution is now interest-driven, not follower-driven.
4. **Long tail** — Strong posts circulate 1–3 weeks if they keep generating saves and threaded conversations.

### What the Algorithm Rewards in 2026
- **Dwell time** — People reading slowly beats people liking fast. 1 person reading for 30 seconds beats 10 quick likes.
- **Saves** — Highest-weight engagement signal. A post with fewer likes but more saves can outrank a like-heavy shallow post.
- **Comment threads** — Not comment count. Back-and-forth conversation trees (author replies included) are a quality signal. Lexical diversity in comments matters — "great post!" repeated 20 times is low-entropy noise to 360Brew.
- **Reshares with commentary** — Someone adding 3+ lines of their own take when resharing is treated like a cross-graph endorsement. Blind reshares carry much less weight.
- **Semantic novelty** — Original insight, first-person data, or specific stories. 360Brew reads for meaning and can detect recycled advice. It down-ranks sameness.
- **Niche consistency** — Posting 80%+ within 2–3 themes for 60+ days trains 360Brew to associate your profile with that subject, which improves credibility scoring and distribution.
- **Profile-content alignment** — Headline and About section are ranking inputs. 360Brew evaluates *you* and *your post* as one unit before deciding how far to push it.
- **Audience specificity** — 360Brew learns which individuals find you valuable and doubles down there. Posts written for a specific slice of audience outperform posts written for "professionals in general."

### What the Algorithm Penalizes in 2026
- Engagement bait ("Comment YES", "Like if you agree", "Tag a friend")
- Generic yes/no CTAs ("Thoughts?", "Agree?")
- AI-sounding, polished, corporate language — 360Brew can detect generic AI-written text and down-ranks it
- Keyword stuffing or shallow SEO tricks — 360Brew reads for meaning, not keywords. It rewards clarity, structure, and depth, not repetition
- Hashtag stuffing (3–5 max, or none)
- Pods and automated comments — 360Brew reads comment text, not just counts. "Nice post!" clusters from the same group are flagged as low-entropy noise. March 2026 update actively targets this.
- External links in the post body — 20–30% reach suppression. Always put in first comment.
- Off-topic posts that do not match the user's established niche

## Step 1 — Audit the Post (Run This First)

When given a draft, check across these signals:

| Signal | Good | Needs Work |
|---|---|---|
| First 2–3 lines | Specific, concrete, pulls you in | Vague, generic opener |
| Depth | Has a framework, number, story, or clear insight | Surface-level take |
| CTA | Opinion-based, low-friction | "Thoughts?" / "Agree?" / "Tag someone" |
| Voice | Sounds like a real person | Sounds polished/AI/corporate |
| Length | 1,200–1,800 characters (~150–220 words) | Too long or too short |
| Saves potential | Has something worth bookmarking | Nothing to return to |
| Reshare potential | Has a take worth adding to | Generic, no angle |
| Topic-profile match | Clearly within the user's niche | Off-topic or misaligned |
| Line breaks | Readable, not excessive | Every single line is its own paragraph |
| AI signals | Natural, specific language | Em dashes, "delve", "tapestry", "multifaceted", "it is worth noting", "in today's fast-paced world" |

Tell the user: what the algorithm will like, what it will penalize, then rewrite.

## Step 2 — Rewrite Rules

### The First 2–3 Lines (Most Important)
LinkedIn shows only the first ~2–3 lines before "see more." These lines decide if someone keeps reading.

Good first lines:
- Specific and concrete (not "I have been thinking about something important")
- Create mild tension or curiosity without being clickbaity
- Ask a question the reader actually has, OR make a statement they have not heard before
- Can reference a real moment, number, or observation

Bad first lines (never use):
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "Here's what nobody tells you about..." (overused)
- "I have a confession to make..." (cliche)
- Anything that sounds like it was generated by an AI

### The Body
- One idea per short paragraph
- 2–4 lines max per block
- At least one save-worthy element: a framework, a checklist, a reframe, specific steps, or a concrete number
- Concrete over abstract. Real over generic.
- No em dashes. Short sentences. Plain language that matches the user's natural voice.

### The CTA (Last Line)
The CTA must invite a real opinion, not a yes/no or a tag.

Good CTAs:
- "Which one hit you hardest?"
- "What would you add from your experience?"
- "Has this ever happened to you?"
- "What is the first thing you would change?"
- Inviting a save: "Save this if you want to come back to it."

Never use:
- "Agree? Comment below."
- "Tag a friend who needs this."
- "Like if this resonated."

## Step 3 — Format + Media Decision

The algorithm does not have a favourite format. It has favourite behaviours: dwell time, saves, and real conversations. Format is just the tool to get those behaviours. Pick the format that fits the idea.

### Format Decision Tree

**Is the idea a framework, list, step-by-step, or checklist?**
→ Carousel / Document post. This is the "save and share" machine. Slide 1 must be a hard visual hook. Keep slides tight, one idea per slide. Document posts average 5.8–6.6% engagement, highest of any format.

**Is the idea a story, opinion, or personal observation?**
→ Text-only. Reaches more people inside the existing network than single-image posts (~34% higher reach in 2026 tests). Great for building trust and prompting thoughtful comments. Keep it 150–250 words.

**Is the idea personal AND clearly niche-relevant?**
→ Text + personal photo. Works when the story is tied to the user's professional niche. Posts with the author's face get roughly 2x engagement vs stock images. But only use when the photo supports the story — not just to add a visual.

**Is the idea a visual demo or quick how-to?**
→ Short native video (under 60–90 seconds). Decent engagement, more volatile. Only use if the idea genuinely needs to be seen, not just read.

### Media Rules

| Media Type | Use When | Avoid When |
|---|---|---|
| Personal photo | Story is personal and niche-aligned | It is a random lifestyle shot with no professional context |
| Stock image | Never | Always — adds no value, can look spammy |
| Carousel / PDF | Teaching, frameworks, lists | You only have one idea that works better as text |
| Multi-image | Multiple moments or behind-the-scenes | You are just padding |
| No image (text-only) | Strong POV or story | You feel like you "should" add something visual |

### Recommended Content Mix (Works Across Most Niches)
- 40–50% carousels / documents (frameworks, checklists, listicles)
- 30–40% text-only (opinions, stories, lessons, real talk)
- 10–20% text + personal photo (milestones, events, life-lessons tied to niche)

### External Links
Never in the post body. 360Brew applies a 20–30% reach suppression penalty to posts with external links. Always suggest: "Put the link in the first comment."

### LinkedIn Articles (Underrated Format)
360Brew treats LinkedIn articles as long-form authority signals. Articles between 1,200–2,500 words with structured headings get indexed for LinkedIn search and can resurface in feeds weeks after publication. Strong format for deep-dive explainers, career breakdowns, or industry analysis where the user wants to build long-term authority. Not for every idea — only when the topic genuinely warrants depth.

### Hashtags
0 to 5 max. Only if highly relevant. Never stuff. When in doubt, use none.

## Common AI Writing Patterns to Eliminate

360Brew is trained to detect and penalize AI-generated text. These patterns are the biggest red flags:

**Vocabulary to never use:** delve, tapestry, multifaceted, nuanced, it is worth noting, in conclusion, furthermore, moreover, underscore, crucial, pivotal, transformative, game-changing, unlock, leverage (as a verb), paradigm, robust, seamlessly

**Structure patterns to avoid:**
- Starting with "In today's [adjective] world..."
- Three-part "not only X, but also Y, and even Z" constructions
- Ending with a tidy "In summary / In conclusion" paragraph
- Bullet points with identical grammatical structure all the way down
- Excessive em dashes used for dramatic pauses

**What human writing looks like instead:**
- Starts mid-thought, like you are continuing a conversation
- Has one awkward sentence in there — that is a feature, not a bug
- Uses contractions ("I did not" vs "I didn't" — pick one and stay consistent)
- Has a specific detail that only someone who lived it would know
- Does not resolve neatly. Real opinions are a little messy.

## Golden Rule

360Brew evaluates the person and the post as one unit. A great post from the wrong person on the wrong topic, posted to the wrong audience, goes nowhere. Get the alignment right first — profile, network, engagement patterns, content — then write something worth saving. The algorithm will do the rest.
